Insurance for Acute Risk: the 39 companies on the market map

The map is from Buoyant Ventures, a climate venture investor: Alex Behar, Climate Risk Intelligence for Insurance: From Transfer to Reduction, 22 September 2025. It sorts example companies into three columns. The post argues that risk transfer alone is not enough, and asks of its own middle column: “who pays, who benefits, and how do we align incentives across homeowners, HOAs, cities, and carriers?”

Each profile below says what the company is, who pays it, at what unit it works, and whether it performs, records or rewards physical risk reduction.

How to read these. The profiles were compiled in one day from public web sources. “Says” and “claims” mark a company’s own statement. “Not found” means not found in this search. Funding totals often conflict between databases, and both figures are given. Some trade-press pages could not be opened and are cited from search summaries, as marked.

Three logos are uncertain readings: MaxInfo, Root and COVR. Rate filings were not read, so counts of approved filings are the vendors’ own.

What shows up across the set

  • Who pays. About 25 of the 39 are paid by an insurer, reinsurer, broker or claims administrator. Five are paid by property owners. Seven are paid mainly by public bodies, utilities or other corporates.
  • Who bears risk. Only Stand, through its Florida reciprocal. Five others sell insurance on someone else’s capacity.
  • Unit. Almost everything in the underwriting column works at the single building or the portfolio. The companies working at community or landscape scale are the ones paid by public bodies and utilities.
  • Physical work. Only BurnBot does mitigation work itself. Fire Aside, Wuuii, XyloPlan and Mitigrate plan, record or certify work that others do.
  • Who pays for mitigation. Homeowners at the parcel; public agencies and foundations at the landscape. No insurer was found paying for durable mitigation. Insurers were found paying for warnings.
  • From finished work to price. Three routes, all at the single home: carrier filings under California’s mitigation-discount regulation (ZestyAI), the IBHS wildfire designation (reached through Wuuii), and Stand’s stated discounts on unfiled rates.
  • Payment on a measured fact. Parametric triggers appear five times, always for the event. Nothing releases payment on verified completion of mitigation work.
  • Acquired or closed. Seven of the 39 have been acquired or have closed, including all three aerial-imagery vendors. Several of those deals predate the map.

7Analytics

IndependentScores risk

7Analytics is a Norwegian flood-modelling company that sells high-resolution flood hazard data and building-level flood scores. It began by selling to construction and municipal users and now presents property insurers as its main market.

Founded, location, status

Founded 2020 in Bergen, Norway, with a United States office in Boston. Independent and venture-backed. No acquisition, merger or shutdown was found.

Paying customer

Named in 2022 reporting: the Municipality of Bergen, Skanska (construction), Multiconsult (engineering) and StormGeo (partner). The company says property insurers in six countries use its data; none are named. Gen Re is named as a partner in a Germany-wide study with 12 unnamed primary carriers.

Business model

Data licence / software subscription. It does not bear or place insurance risk.

Funding and backers

Seed of $2.5 million (September 2022; Momentum Partners lead, Construct Venture, Obos VC). €4 million from Scale Capital (May 2024). Undisclosed investment from IAG Firemark Ventures (reported 7 February 2025). Aggregator totals conflict: PitchBook $8.35 million, Coverager $6.86 million.

Unit of analysis

Single building, aggregated to portfolio; also used at site and municipal scale.

Product and mechanism

Inputs are digital elevation models, satellite imagery, land-cover and climate/rainfall data, processed with hydrological modelling and machine learning. Outputs are flood hazard layers (the company says at 1 x 1 metre resolution) and a building-level "Pluvial Flood Index". Hazards: pluvial (surface-water) flood first, then riverine, coastal and flash flood; landslide is mentioned. The output feeds insurer underwriting, pricing and event response, and site-level planning for construction and infrastructure owners.

Physical risk reduction

Information only. Municipal and construction customers may use the maps to design drainage, but no mechanism by which mitigation is verified, financed or rewarded in an insurance price was found.

Maturity evidence

Three disclosed funding events; a strategic investment by an insurer's venture arm (IAG); a 2025 industry award (ITC Europe DIAmond). No published customer count, revenue or regulatory acceptance of the model was found.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

Funding rounds and the early named customers are independently reported. The "insurers in six countries" figure, the 1 x 1 metre resolution, and the statement that the model outperforms other flood models and the German ZÜRS zones against claims data are company claims; no methodology was published on the page seen.

Arturo was a property-analytics company that turned aerial, satellite and stratospheric imagery into structured property attributes for insurers. It ceased operating in 2025 after selling its assets.

Founded, location, status

Spun out of American Family Insurance in 2018 (formerly Deep Image Analytics). HQ is reported inconsistently: Chicago at launch, Denver in most later profiles, South Jordan, Utah in The Insurer's 2025 report. Status: wound down. The Insurer reported on 30 July 2025 that Arturo had ceased operations after an asset sale, citing the chief executive; Coverager reported the website offline and the sale completed "a couple of months" earlier. The buyer was not named. PitchBook lists the company as out of business.

Paying customer

Insurers. Reported clients: American Family, USAA, Openly, Hippo, IAG and Suncorp.

Business model

Data licence / API subscription. No risk bearing.

Funding and backers

About $47 million in total per Coverager. Series B of $25 million in 2021 led by Atlantic Bridge; a CB Insights entry shows $13.7 million on 12 September 2023. Investors: American Family, Atlantic Bridge, Crosslink Capital, IAG Firemark Ventures, RPS Ventures.

Unit of analysis

Single parcel/building.

Product and mechanism

Inputs were third-party imagery (Vexcel, Near Space Labs, Urban Sky and others). Computer-vision models produced attributes such as roof material and condition, pools, trampolines, tree overhang and building footprint. Outputs fed quote-time underwriting, renewal review and post-event claims triage. Hazards: wind, hail, wildfire exposure indirectly through property condition.

Physical risk reduction

Information only.

Maturity evidence

Reached named tier-one insurer customers on three continents, then failed as a standalone company: three chief executive changes by September 2023, an asset sale and cessation in 2025.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

The closure is confirmed by two trade outlets, but both rely on the chief executive's own LinkedIn post. The statement that the technology "will continue" in earth observation for large insurers is the former chief executive's assertion; the acquirer and terms are not found.

Betterview

Acquired by NearmapScores risk

Betterview is a property-intelligence software platform for property and casualty insurers. Since the Nearmap acquisition it is a product line of Nearmap rather than a company.

Founded, location, status

Founded 2014 in San Francisco as a drone roof-inspection service. Nearmap announced an agreement to acquire it on 5 December 2023; terms undisclosed. A separate completion notice was not found, but Nearmap releases from October 2024 onward describe "the Betterview platform by Nearmap" as an existing product. Nearmap itself is a Thoma Bravo portfolio company (taken private in 2022). Nearmap later bought Tensorflight's assets (October 2024) and itel.

Paying customer

Insurers. Carrier selections listed in Nearmap's newsroom include Augusta Mutual, Utica First, Allied Trust, Ohio Mutual and Franklin Mutual. In 2020 Betterview said it had twenty carriers in production.

Business model

SaaS subscription. No risk bearing.

Funding and backers (before acquisition)

Totals conflict: $17 million as of the June 2020 round per the company; PitchBook lists $33.8 million; CB Insights $16.36 million. Investors: Nationwide, EMC Insurance, Guidewire, Maiden Re, ManchesterStory. Last round January 2023, amount undisclosed.

Unit of analysis

Single parcel/building, with portfolio views.

Product and mechanism

Inputs are aerial imagery (now mainly Nearmap's own) plus public records and third-party peril data. Computer vision produces more than 100 property attributes (company figure) and roof and peril vulnerability scores. Outputs sit in an underwriter workflow tool and flag properties for action, inspection or straight-through processing at new business and renewal. Hazards: hail, wind, wildfire, general roof condition.

Physical risk reduction

Information only. The tool can prompt an insurer to ask a policyholder to repair a roof or clear vegetation; no third-party-documented discount or verification mechanism was found.

Maturity evidence

Acquired by a larger imagery company; integration into Guidewire PolicyCenter announced 27 October 2025; a steady list of small and regional mutual carrier customers. Current customer count not found.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

BirdsEyeView

IndependentScores risk

BirdsEyeView is a small London insurtech that sells weather-risk analytics and an automated quote-and-bind engine to London-market insurers and brokers, originally for event-cancellation ("contingency") and parametric weather cover.

Founded, location, status

Founded 2019, London. Lloyd's Lab accelerator cohort 7 (2021). Independent. No acquisition or shutdown found; no dated 2025 or 2026 funding news found. The European Space Agency project page was updated 12 September 2025 and describes the product as in live commercial use.

Paying customer

Insurers and brokers. Arch Insurance is named by ESA as the pilot customer with an extended agreement. A December 2023 report names Liberty Specialty, Arch, Convex, Ark, Everest Re and Fidelis as "strategic partnerships"; whether all pay is not found.

Business model

Software licence. Early coverage described it as structuring parametric cover for small businesses, but no evidence was found that it holds delegated underwriting authority or places risk on named capacity. Classification as an MGA is unconfirmed; the evidence points to a software vendor.

Funding and backers

Seed in June 2022 led by SFC Capital, matched by ESA, amount undisclosed. A "seven-figure" round in December 2023 with ESA, Big 5 Investors, InsurTech NY and ACF Investors. Total not found.

Unit of analysis

A location and date (an event or site); portfolio exposure for the insurer.

Product and mechanism

Inputs are ECMWF ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalysis data from Copernicus, plus historical natural-catastrophe data. "WEATHER ANALYTIX" outputs the probability of adverse weather at a given location and date, used by an underwriter to price and to manage exposure. "RAPTOR" lets a broker configure, quote and bind low-premium policies, referring out-of-appetite risks to an underwriter. Hazards: rain, wind and other adverse weather for events; wider natural catastrophe claimed.

Physical risk reduction

No. Information and pricing only.

Maturity evidence

Lloyd's lists headcount at 1 to 10. ESA says contracts worth "hundreds of thousands of euros" with 14 insurers and brokers. No premium volume, policy count or loss data found.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

ESA and Lloyd's pages are third-party but largely restate company-supplied information. Customer names beyond Arch rest on a press release. Evidence is thin for 2024 to 2026.

CAPE Analytics

Acquired by Moody'sScores risk

CAPE Analytics produces property attributes and risk scores from aerial imagery using computer vision, sold to insurers and lenders. It is now part of Moody's.

Founded, location, status

Founded 2014, Mountain View, California. Moody's announced the acquisition on 13 January 2025, terms undisclosed, with closing expected in the first quarter of 2025. MarketScreener records completion in January 2025 and PitchBook gives 15 January 2025; Moody's own closing statement was not retrieved. The capeanalytics.com site now redirects to Moody's property-underwriting pages. PitchBook lists a deal value of $211 million, which is not a company disclosure.

Paying customer

Insurers and some banks. CAPE said nearly half of the top 50 United States property insurers were customers. Named partners in earlier releases include Mercury, Cypress and Kin.

Business model

Data licence / API. No risk bearing.

Funding and backers (before acquisition)

About $75 million. Series C of $44 million (July 2021, Pivot Investment Partners); Series B of $17 million (2018, XL Innovate); State Farm Ventures (2020); The Hartford, Nephila, CSAA, Cincinnati Insurance, State Auto, Hudson Structured.

Unit of analysis

Single parcel/building.

Product and mechanism

Inputs are aerial and satellite imagery from third-party providers plus public records. Outputs include Roof Condition Rating, roof age, vegetation and defensible-space measures, yard debris and other attributes. They feed quoting, rating plans, underwriting eligibility and renewal review. Hazards: wind, hail, wildfire. Moody's states it is combining these with its catastrophe models (formerly RMS).

Physical risk reduction

Information only, but with a regulatory footprint: the Roof Condition Rating is a filed rating variable, so a roof in better condition can receive a lower rate where a carrier's filing uses it. CAPE and Moody's say the rating is approved for ratemaking in more than 39 states, with over 300 approved filings; a Duck Creek brochure says 41 states. Those counts are vendor statements about regulator filings; individual filings were not checked here. CAPE does not verify or finance repairs.

Maturity evidence

Acquired by a public ratings and risk-model company; insurer-investors who were also customers; state-approved use in rate filings (count as claimed).

Self-described vs independently confirmed

The acquisition is confirmed by Moody's investor-relations release and trade press. The "half of the top 50" and state-approval counts are company claims.

Delos

(Delos Insurance Solutions)
IndependentInsures or triggers a payout

Delos is a managing general agent that sells homeowners insurance in wildfire-exposed parts of California, selecting homes with its own wildfire model. It does not carry the risk itself.

Founded, location, status

Founded 2017 in San Francisco (Dealroom says 2016). Independent and operating. Named MGA/MGU of the Year by Insurance Insider US in September 2024.

Paying customer

The homeowner pays premium, through independent brokers and agents (the company says about 14,000 in California). Delos earns from the carriers it writes for.

Business model

MGA commission on surplus-lines (non-admitted) business. Paper: Canopius US Insurance, Inc. since launch in June 2020. The Insurer reports Arch Re and a Lloyd's syndicate added as reinsurance support behind Canopius, and Lloyd's syndicates for the vacant-property product (paywalled; via search summary). A consumer review site also lists Homesite and Summit, unverified.

Funding and backers

$5.3 million seed (December 2021). $9 million Series A (31 October 2024) led by HSBC Asset Management with IA Capital, Blue Bear Capital and Generation Space.

Unit of analysis

Single parcel, within a landscape-scale fire model.

Product and mechanism

Inputs are satellite imagery, wind maps, precipitation and vegetation-moisture data, developed with Spatial Informatics Group. The model outputs a wildfire risk view per address, which decides whether Delos will offer a policy and at what price. Delos says it accepts about 65 percent of business the primary market declines for wildfire. Hazard: wildfire. Products: HO-3 for primary, secondary and seasonal homes, and vacant-home policies.

Physical risk reduction

Mostly selection. Delos says it sends policyholders notices about changing risk and guidance on home hardening. One consumer article reports that fireproofing is a factor in eligibility but that Delos does not offer specific mitigation discounts. As surplus-lines business, its rates are not filed with the California Department of Insurance, so no regulator filing documents any mitigation credit.

Maturity evidence

The company says it has $40 billion of insured value (October 2024), a 25,000-policy portfolio, eligibility across about 12 million California homes (April 2026), and zero wildfire losses from the January 2025 Los Angeles fires because it was not writing in the burned areas. Premium volume and loss ratios are not published.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

Funding rounds, the Canopius relationship and the award are independently reported. Policy counts, insured value, eligibility counts, the zero-loss statement and "market-leading loss ratios" are company statements repeated by trade press.

Demex

(The Demex Group)
IndependentInsures or triggers a payout

Demex is a Washington, D.C. reinsurance MGA and analytics firm that designs aggregate reinsurance for insurers' frequent storm losses, settled against a modelled-loss index instead of adjusted claims.

Founded, location, status

Formed 2019 per its 2022 NAIC presentation (CB Insights says 2020). Independent. It pivoted: 2021 to 2022 products were climate-risk appraisals and snow cover for businesses and asset owners; since about 2023 it sells only to insurers. A new chief executive was named in December 2025.

Paying customer

Primary insurers, mainly regional and mutual carriers, through reinsurance brokers. Named: Innovated Holdings (CFM Insurance, Forreston Mutual), placed by Acrisure Re.

Business model

Demex structures and models the cover and acts as MGA; reinsurers bear the risk. Capacity providers are described only as "A-rated" and are not named. Demex's exact legal role in each placement was not confirmed.

Funding and backers

$9 million Series A (December 2021); $5 million (August 2023); $10.25 million (September 2024, "Series A and SAFE", led by Congruent Ventures with Moxxie, MetaProp, Blue Bear). Earlier backers: Anthemis, IA Capital, QBE Ventures. CB Insights total: $28.45 million. No 2025 or 2026 equity round found.

Unit of analysis

An insurer's portfolio.

Product and mechanism

Inputs are a carrier's own ground-up claims for roughly ten years (the company says more than $30 billion in total across carriers) overlaid with weather observations. The output is a carrier-specific modelled-loss index. When the index for the season exceeds an attachment point, the reinsurer pays the carrier. The decision it feeds is whether a carrier can buy working-layer aggregate cover that the traditional market withdrew. Hazards: severe convective storm (hail, tornado, straight-line wind); a winter-storm version launched June 2026.

Physical risk reduction

No. It is risk transfer between insurer and reinsurer.

Maturity evidence

$65 million of limit bound in the first season (company, 2024); more than $75 million of 2025 limits; more than $140 million of cover bought through six brokers by December 2025; payouts reported for 2025 in several states, including by the named client. The "$500 million capacity" figure refers to reinsurer capacity under discussion, not cover bound.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

Funding and the Innovated Holdings placement are independently reported, with the client quoted. Limit totals, payout statements and the claim that industry data misses 15 to 50 percent of storm losses are company figures.

Faura

Independent, seedPlans or records others' work

Faura is an early-stage company that scores how likely an individual structure is to survive a disaster and gives insurers a homeowner-facing assessment and mitigation plan.

Founded, location, status

Founded 2023. HQ is reported inconsistently: Las Vegas (PitchBook), Napa (VCBacked), New York area (one aggregator). Independent, seed stage. No acquisition or shutdown found.

Paying customer

Insurers and MGAs. No customer is named in any source found. Distribution through Insurity's policy platform was announced 7 October 2025.

Business model

Software/data licence. Pricing not found. No risk bearing.

Funding and backers

$500,000 pre-seed (April 2024). $3.5 million seed (February 2025) co-led by Harlem Capital and Building Ventures, with MetaProp, Triple Five and Dorm Room Fund. Total about $4.4 million.

Unit of analysis

Single building.

Product and mechanism

Inputs are third-party structure data and a guided digital self-assessment completed by the homeowner (building materials, vents, defensible space and similar features). These are compared with how similar features performed in past events. Outputs are a per-property resilience or survivability score for the underwriter and a mitigation "roadmap" for the homeowner. The score feeds eligibility, pricing and renewal decisions. Hazards: wildfire first; investors say hurricane, hail, flood and earthquake are also covered.

Physical risk reduction

Partly, by design. The product tells a homeowner what to change and lets an insurer record homeowner-reported features. Press material refers to "possible premium incentives", but no named carrier, discount schedule or regulator filing recognising a Faura score was found. Verification is by homeowner self-report through the guided assessment; independent inspection was not found. Faura does not finance or perform the work.

Maturity evidence

Two small rounds; pitch-competition wins (State Farm at InsureTech Connect, Guidewire); one platform integration. No customer count, revenue or filed use.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

Funding is confirmed by an investor's own post and trade press. The 90 to 95 percent accuracy figure for the Mountain, Palisades and Eaton fires, the existence of paying carrier clients, and the premium-incentive mechanism are company statements. Evidence is thin.

Fathom

Acquired by Swiss ReScores risk

Fathom is a flood-modelling firm from Bristol, United Kingdom, that produces global flood hazard maps, flood catastrophe models and terrain data. It is a wholly owned Swiss Re business that keeps its own brand.

Founded, location, status

Founded 2013 out of the University of Bristol's hydrology group (some sources say 2012). Swiss Re announced the acquisition on 13 to 14 December 2023, price undisclosed, into its Reinsurance Solutions division. Moody's had been a shareholder and ceased to be. Still operating under the Fathom name in November 2026, with releases through the year.

Paying customer

Insurers, reinsurers, brokers, engineering firms, banks, governments and development bodies. Documented: Aon Impact Forecasting (collaboration), ERM, Texas Water Development Board, WSP, Jacobs (Flood Modeller), CARTO, Climate X. Distribution through Moody's Intelligent Risk Platform and Swiss Re's CatNet. Fathom's United States model underlay First Street Foundation's Flood Factor in 2020.

Business model

Data and model licence, direct and through resellers. Fathom bears no risk; its owner Swiss Re does, and since January 2026 is integrating Fathom data into its own internal flood model.

Funding and backers

No venture rounds found; Moody's (via RMS) held a stake before the sale. Total raised: not found.

Unit of analysis

Grid cell (about 30 metres globally, finer nationally), aggregable from single location to portfolio, catchment and country.

Product and mechanism

Inputs are terrain data (its own FathomDEM+), river-gauge and rainfall records and climate-model output, run through hydrodynamic simulation. Outputs are flood depth and extent maps by return period (Global, United States, United Kingdom, Japan), risk scores, and probabilistic catastrophe models on the Oasis framework that give loss distributions. They feed underwriting and accumulation control for insurers, capital modelling for reinsurers, climate disclosure for corporates and banks, and planning for public agencies. Hazards: fluvial, pluvial and coastal flood.

Physical risk reduction

Information only. Public-sector users such as the Texas Water Development Board use the data for flood planning, so it informs mitigation siting, but Fathom does not verify or finance works, and no insurance pricing credit is tied to it.

Maturity evidence

Acquisition by a top-tier reinsurer; peer-reviewed academic origins; use in a state flood plan; adoption into Swiss Re's internal catastrophe model (announced 13 January 2026); a free United Kingdom catastrophe model released September 2026. Headcount and revenue not found. No regulator approval of the model was found.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

The acquisition is confirmed by Swiss Re and trade press. Customer statements such as "many of the world's largest insurers" are Fathom's own; the named partners above appear on Fathom's site and in some cases on the partner's own site (Jacobs, First Street).

FloodFlash is a London managing general agent (MGA) and Lloyd's coverholder that sells parametric flood insurance to commercial properties. A depth sensor fixed to the building triggers a pre-agreed payout.

Founded, location, status

Founded in 2017 in London. Acquired by NormanMax Insurance Holdings (Miami, a parametric specialist that operates Lloyd's Syndicate 3939, managed by Apollo). The agreement was announced on 26 February 2025, subject to Financial Conduct Authority approval; NormanMax issued a release dated 20 May 2025 announcing the acquisition. Terms were not disclosed. A search summary of a report in The Insurer says a co-founder left and a NormanMax Syndicate 3939 underwriter became chief underwriting officer on 1 March 2026 (the page itself could not be fetched).

Paying customer

Commercial property owners and businesses, reached through brokers. The company insures only commercial properties.

Business model

MGA commission on parametric policies. FloodFlash does not bear the risk. Capacity has come from Munich Re (UK book since 2020, US extension March 2023, mainland-US-wide through the Munich Re syndicate in June 2024) and Hiscox (US, August 2023). Insurers. MetLife is documented: first engaged in 2023, with an expansion from Latin America to the US and Asia announced 25 June 2025. Zurich is named in older coverage.

Software licence, likely volume-linked; pricing not found.

WeatherMind

Independent, pre-seedNo mitigation role

A Paris startup that assesses residential property damage from policyholder photos and video for insurers, with a human adjuster validating each result.

Founded, location, status

Founded 2023; Paris. Independent, pre-seed per an Antler listing. Spelled both WeatherMind and Weathermind.

Paying customer

Insurers and loss-adjusting firms in France. One unnamed large French insurer is described as a subsidence client. No named customer found.

Business model

Not found; the subsidence example cites €150 per assessment, which suggests a per-claim fee.

Funding and backers

About $160K over three accelerator-type rounds per CB Insights, latest October 2023. Backers named by aggregators: Antler, Plug and Play Insurtech Europe, Centre Street Partners. No priced round found.

Product and mechanism

The policyholder uploads live-captured, geolocated photos or video; the software classifies the damage, judges whether the images support the reported cause, estimates repair cost under the insurer's pricing and policy terms, and produces a settlement report that an adjuster validates in about 15 minutes, per a September 2025 conference write-up. Low-value claims are settled remotely and complex ones are passed to field experts. It changes triage, remote inspection and estimating. The site names a product, HomeLens, for flood, storm and glass claims.

Relation to catastrophe events

The closest in this column to a catastrophe-specific product. The name and the original description refer to natural disasters, and the documented perils are subsidence (a drought-driven peril covered by the French natural-catastrophe regime), flood and storm, alongside escape of water. No named event deployment found.

Prevention or mitigation

No. It acts after a loss is reported.

Maturity evidence

Thin. One to ten employees. The company says subsidence assessments fall from up to six months to three days and that cause identification is 85 to 90 percent reliable. UK entry was "under discussion" in 2025.

Self-described vs independently confirmed

Nearly everything rests on the company's site, aggregator listings and one conference summary based on the company's own presentation.
